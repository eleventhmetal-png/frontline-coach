# Competitor — North (northcoach.net)

Researched 2026-07-27 from their marketing site.

**This is the direct competitor.** frontline.coach is a different audience (sales reps).
Peopling is a different audience (tech managers) and a single feature. North is aimed at
the same person Frontline Coach is aimed at, with a broader product.

Company: "North Coach", CVR 46406087 — a Danish business registration. No named founder
anywhere on the site.

## Their stated audience, in their own words

> "built for frontline and mid-level managers — store managers, team leads, hospitality
> GMs, healthcare supervisors, restaurant managers, and engineering managers"

That is Frontline Coach's audience plus engineering managers.

They also run per-industry pages with real KPI breakdowns: `/for/retail`,
`/for/hospitality`, `/for/healthcare`, `/for/technology`, `/for/qsr`, `/for/general`.
Retail page lists conversion rate, UPT, ATV, NPS, attachment rate, shrinkage. That's the
same industry-layer concept already in `App.jsx`, built out as public content.

## Feature overlap

Things they have that Frontline Coach also has:

- Difficult conversation coaching, context-aware
- Knows your team — per-person memory, notes, history ("North remembers what you said
  about them")
- Industry-specific adaptation
- Free tier

Things they have that Frontline Coach does not:

- **Voice coaching.** Shipped. Their words: *"Talk to your coach and listen to responses
  with natural AI voices — no typing needed, perfect for on-the-go."* That is precisely
  the argument for voice on a frontline floor, and they got there first.
- **Photo to coaching.** Snap a picture of meeting notes or a whiteboard and it coaches
  from that.
- **Daily huddle generator.** Produces a morning shift script with campaign and KPI
  references. Genuinely frontline-shaped and a smart wedge.
- **Coaching playbooks** — step-by-step guides with AI at each step. Note: this is
  exactly what Ben's Team Leads asked for ("guide the conversation one step at a time").
- 1-on-1 tracker with prep cards, per-report pulse (green/yellow/red), coaching journal,
  weekly digest, courses with lessons, 16 conversation templates, skip-level coaching,
  meeting timer, PDF export, themes.

Things Frontline Coach has that North does not:

- **Roleplay / Practice against an AI employee.** I see no rehearsal feature anywhere on
  their site. They have chat coaching and courses. This is now Frontline Coach's clearest
  product differentiator against the closest competitor.
- **Pushback Coach** — dedicated in-the-moment wording while somebody is in front of you.
- **Skill vs. Will diagnostic.**
- **Documentation Assistant.** They offer "share notes with HR"; they don't generate a
  factual record from rough notes.
- **A named human with operator credibility.**

## Their pricing (useful anchor)

| Tier | Price | Limits |
|---|---|---|
| Free | $0 | 15 AI messages/day, 2 team members |
| Pro | $15/mo | Unlimited messages and team members, all features |
| Pro Annual | $12/mo ($144/yr) | Same |
| Team | $19/user/mo | Adds unlimited skip-level |

This corrects my earlier guess that a personally-paying frontline supervisor caps out in
single digits. Somebody is pricing this audience at $15/month. Whether they're converting
at that price is unknown — but it's a real anchor and it's higher than I assumed.

## Where they're weak

**The testimonials look manufactured.** Three quotes, initials-only surnames, one
conveniently per industry — "Alex M., Retail Store Manager", "Sarah K., Engineering
Manager", "James T., Hotel GM". Generic phrasing, no company, no specificity. The product
screenshots use stock names too (Sarah Chen, Marcus, Maria Rodriguez, David Kim). A
careful reader discounts all of it.

**No founder, no story, no operator.** Nobody's name is on the site. There's no "why I
built this." A Danish company registration is coaching American shift leads with no
stated connection to the work. That's a real credibility hole and it's the one thing Ben
has that cannot be copied.

**Very broad surface for a small team.** Sixteen templates, courses, playbooks, journal,
pulse, huddles, photo-to-coaching, three themes, PDF export. Breadth like that usually
means thin execution somewhere, and it's a hard product to keep coherent.

**Audience dilution.** Including engineering managers, sprint velocity and MTTR alongside
retail shrinkage means their coaching has to speak two languages. Their own demo shows a
performance conversation about missed sprint deadlines and story points. That is not a
shift lead's world.

## The honest strategic read

**Frontline Coach cannot out-feature North.** They're ahead on product breadth, they have
voice, and they have a build velocity that suggests more than one person. Trying to match
the feature list is the losing move — it's a race Ben would run second in while
abandoning what makes his product distinct.

**Out-specify them instead.** Three assets North structurally cannot copy:

1. **Rehearsal.** They coach you on what to say. Frontline Coach lets you practise it
   against somebody who pushes back. That's the gap between advice and reps, and it's the
   thing Ben's own users engaged with second-most.
2. **The operator story.** Eighteen years, a Navy carrier, a woman whose job he cost by
   assuming she knew. An anonymous Danish LLC cannot manufacture that, and for a
   frontline supervisor deciding who to trust, it's decisive.
3. **The hourly floor, specifically.** No engineering managers, no sprint velocity, no
   story points. When North writes a performance conversation example it involves missed
   sprints. When Frontline Coach writes one it involves a prep who said "that's not my
   job" in front of two coworkers. That specificity is the whole product.

**What to actually do**

- **Voice moves from differentiator to table stakes.** Two of three competitors now have
  it. Still gated behind billing for cost reasons, but the framing changes: it's no
  longer a feature that wins, it's a gap that loses. Sequence unchanged, urgency higher.
- **Lead with Practice everywhere.** It's currently one of six tools on the landing page.
  Against the closest competitor it's the only thing they don't have. It deserves more
  prominence than equal billing.
- **The step-by-step playbook request now has competitive weight.** Ben's Team Leads asked
  for it; North shipped it. It moved from a nice-to-have to a known gap.
- **Consider per-industry pages.** North has six. Ben has zero, and the app already has
  industry layers built. This is a content play he's already 80% equipped for. Open
  question for Ben: he ruled out naming any industry publicly to protect job separation —
  but restaurant, retail, warehouse and hospitality pages don't identify his employer.
  Worth revisiting whether the constraint was "no car wash" or "no industries at all."
- **Do not build a daily huddle generator to match.** It's good, but it's their wedge and
  it pulls away from the conversation-quality focus that makes this product coherent.

## Bottom line

North is the competitor to watch and the one most likely to be the answer an AI gives
today, because they have more indexed content, more features, and paid acquisition
(they're running a marketing pixel).

They're also faceless, their social proof looks fabricated, and they're trying to serve
shift leads and engineering managers with one product. Frontline Coach is narrower,
slower, and has a real person behind it who did the job badly for years and can prove it.

Narrow and credible beats broad and anonymous with this audience — but only if the
narrowness is obvious within five seconds of landing.
