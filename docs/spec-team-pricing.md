# Team pricing — parked 27 July 2026

Not for publication. Revisit once there are external users and at least the roster
and shared-library features exist. Pinned at Ben's request.

## Don't publish a team tier yet

Zero external users, and no team features exist. Ten seats today = ten identical
individual accounts, so a company gets nothing that ten people expensing $14.99
wouldn't. A public tier you can't service is a credibility problem, not revenue.

North's own team uplift is $19 vs $15 — only $4 — which suggests their team tier is
thin as well.

## The blocking decision is privacy, not price

Employer-bought seats can break the product. It works because a supervisor will type
*"I'm afraid to have this conversation"* or *"I think I handled that badly."* Once
their district manager is paying, they assume the district manager can read it, and
they stop typing the true thing. The result is a tool people perform into rather than
think in.

Posture to commit to before selling a single seat:

- Buyer sees **seat count, active users, sessions run.** Never content, never who was
  coached, never what was said.
- Say it in the Terms and on the pricing page in plain words: *your manager can see
  that you used it, never what you said.*

This is also a wedge against North. An org-chart product can't credibly promise it.

## What a team plan must contain to be worth buying

Otherwise it's a discount with a new name.

- One invoice, seat add/remove, no expense reports
- Aggregate usage for the buyer — adoption, not surveillance
- Org-level industry setting so every seat speaks the same operation's language
- **Shared scenario / policy library** — the org's own escalation rules and
  documentation standard. This is the one that makes it enterprise: a company doesn't
  want generic coaching, it wants coaching that matches its own policy.
- Roster (already required regardless — see the same-name merge bug in ROADMAP § 3)

## Numbers

**$19/user/month, 5-seat minimum, annual commit. Team gets Premium capability.**

| Tier | Price |
|---|---|
| Standard | $14.99 |
| Premium | $24.99 |
| **Team** | **$19/seat, 5 seat min., annual** |
| 25+ seats | $16 |
| 100+ seats | $13, invoiced |

Why $19: matches North, so Frontline Coach is never the expensive option in a
side-by-side. Above Standard, so it isn't a downgrade path. Below individual
Premium, which is correct — that's a volume discount. The 5-seat minimum and annual
commit are what stop two people forming a "team" to get Premium for $19.

## The ROI argument

Turnover, not training budget. $19 × 12 = **$228/year per supervisor.** Ben knows the
real cost to replace one frontline employee; use that number. If the tool prevents one
avoidable exit per site per year the math isn't close, and that's the only argument an
ops buyer responds to.
